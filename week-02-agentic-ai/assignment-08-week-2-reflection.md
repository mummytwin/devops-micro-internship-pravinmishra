```
What I Learned About Agentic AI and DevOps

My understanding of DevOps changed fundamentally this week. I used to think DevOps was about writing scripts and managing servers. Now I see it as a system of workflows  and Agentic AI fits directly into that system.
What surprised me most is that control matters as much as capability. The hooks and permissions assignment showed me that giving AI unrestricted access to infrastructure is dangerous. The principle of least privilege giving each agent only the tools it actually needs is not just a security concept. It is a design philosophy that applies to AI tools, IAM roles, Kubernetes RBAC, and Linux file permissions equally.

P.S. This post is a part of DevOps Micro Internship with Agentic AI Cohort-3 by Pravin Mishra. You can start your DevOps journey by joining this Discord community ( [https://lnkd.in/eQRdCjm2) ).
```

One System I Will Implement From This Week

Before starting every assignment, I will read all the screenshot requirements first and create a checklist in my notebook before writing a single command. I will do this at the beginning of each study session so I never miss proof at a critical step. This will save me from having to scroll back through long terminal outputs trying to find a response I should have captured immediately.

Biggest Insight I Got About Myself This Week

I learned that I am a hands-on learner. Reading instructions alone does not stick for me but the moment I run a command, see an error, fix it, and watch it work, the concept becomes permanent. I also noticed that I sometimes move too fast and skip verification steps, which caused me to miss screenshots at the right moment. I learned to slow down and capture proof before moving to the next task.

My Biggest Weakness or Loop I Noticed

My biggest repeated challenge was accidentally exposing sensitive information. Twice during Assignment 5, I pasted my GitHub Personal Access Token in the wrong place once in the terminal and once in a commit. GitHub caught it and blocked the push, which taught me a valuable lesson: secret management is not optional. I now understand why settings.local.json must always be gitignored and why tokens must only ever live in secure, non-committed files.

My Week 2 Highlight

My proudest moment was when the PreToolUse hook blocked terraform destroy. I had built the hook myself, configured it in settings.json, and when Claude attempted to run the destructive command, it was stopped cold. Claude even explained transparently that it had hit a guardrail I had intentionally put in place and offered three safe alternatives instead.That moment made me feel like a real DevOps engineer.
